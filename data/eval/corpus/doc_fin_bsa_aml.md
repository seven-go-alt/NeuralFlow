# BSA/AML Compliance Requirements and Penalties under the Bank Secrecy Act

## Overview of the Bank Secrecy Act

The **Bank Secrecy Act** (BSA), also known as the Currency and Foreign Transactions Reporting Act, is the primary US federal law governing anti-money laundering compliance. The BSA requires financial institutions to assist government agencies in detecting and preventing money laundering by establishing **record keeping** and reporting requirements. Key obligations include filing Currency Transaction Reports for transactions exceeding $10,000, filing Suspicious Activity Reports for certain suspicious transactions, and maintaining adequate anti-money laundering programs. The **Anti-Money Laundering** Act of 2020 significantly expanded the scope and enforcement authority of the BSA.

## Record Keeping Requirements Under the BSA

The BSA imposes specific record keeping requirements on financial institutions, including banks, broker-dealers, money services businesses, and other covered entities. These requirements include maintaining records of currency transactions, retaining copies of funds transfer instructions, and preserving customer identification information obtained through Customer Due Diligence procedures. The BSA's record keeping provisions are designed to create a paper trail that law enforcement and regulatory authorities can use to investigate financial crimes. Financial institutions must retain these records for a minimum of five years and produce them upon request by regulatory authorities.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Non-compliance with **BSA**/AML record keeping requirements can result in severe **penalties**. Civil penalties under the BSA can reach **up to the greater of $1,063,633 per violation** (this amount is indexed annually for inflation) or the value of the transaction or account involved. Criminal penalties are even more severe, with fines of up to $1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years for willful violations of the BSA's record keeping and reporting requirements. The exact civil penalty amount is adjusted annually by the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act, and financial institutions should verify the current indexed amount for compliance purposes.

## The Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020

The **Anti-Money Laundering Act** of 2020, enacted as part of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, represents the most significant overhaul of the US anti-money laundering framework since the USA PATRIOT Act. Key provisions of the 2020 Act include the expansion of beneficial ownership information reporting, enhanced whistleblower protections and awards, expanded subpoena authority for the Treasury Department, and modernization of the BSA's outdated provisions. The Act also mandated the establishment of a centralized Beneficial Ownership Registry and directed the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) to issue regulations implementing these reforms. The expanded enforcement authority under the 2020 Act has led to increased scrutiny of financial institutions' BSA/AML compliance programs.

## Compliance Best Practices

To mitigate the risk of penalties, Meridian Analytics maintains a comprehensive BSA/AML compliance program that includes a designated Compliance Officer, written policies and procedures, ongoing employee training, independent testing and audit, and risk-based Customer Due Diligence procedures. Regular compliance reviews and updates to the anti-money laundering program are essential to address evolving regulatory requirements and emerging money laundering typologies. Financial institutions should also ensure that their record keeping systems are capable of maintaining the required documentation for the statutory retention periods and can respond promptly to regulatory inquiries and examinations.
