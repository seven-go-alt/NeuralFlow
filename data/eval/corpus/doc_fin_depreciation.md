# Depreciation Policy for Computing Equipment Under US GAAP

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-001
**Owner:** Corporate Accounting Policy Team
**Last Updated:** July 15, 2026

## Overview

Meridian Analytics maintains fixed asset accounting policies in accordance with US GAAP, specifically ASC 360-10 (Property, Plant, and Equipment). This memorandum documents the depreciation method and useful life applied to computing equipment, which constitutes a significant portion of Meridian's capital expenditure given the company's data analytics infrastructure needs.

## Depreciation Method

Meridian Analytics uses the **straight-line method** for all computing equipment under US GAAP. The straight-line method is selected because it most faithfully represents the pattern in which the asset's economic benefits are consumed over its service life. Under this method, the cost of the asset, less any estimated residual value, is charged to depreciation expense evenly over each accounting period of the asset's useful life.

The straight-line method is applied consistently to all classes of computing equipment, including servers, workstations, networking hardware, and peripheral devices. Meridian does not employ accelerated depreciation methods such as double-declining balance or sum-of-the-years'-digits for computing equipment, as the consumption pattern of these assets does not warrant such treatment.

## Standard Useful Life

The standard useful life for computing equipment at Meridian Analytics is **5 years**. This estimate is based on the company's historical experience with technology refresh cycles, vendor support timelines, and the expected period over which these assets will remain productive for the business.

Computing equipment is grouped into the following subcategories, all with a 5-year useful life:

- **Servers and data processing hardware:** 5 years
- **Desktop and laptop workstations:** 5 years
- **Networking and telecommunications equipment:** 5 years
- **Peripheral devices:** 5 years

## Residual Value

Meridian estimates residual value at zero for all computing equipment, as the expected proceeds from disposition at the end of the 5-year useful life are immaterial. The zero-residual-value assumption is consistent with industry practice for technology assets that rapidly lose value due to obsolescence.

## Capitalization Threshold

Computing equipment with a unit cost of $2,500 or more is capitalized and depreciated over the 5-year useful life. Items below this threshold are expensed as incurred under Meridian's de minimis policy. The capitalization threshold is reviewed annually by the Corporate Accounting Policy Team.

## Conclusion

Meridian Analytics applies the straight-line depreciation method over a 5-year useful life for all capitalized computing equipment under US GAAP. This policy ensures consistent, systematic cost allocation that aligns with the expected pattern of economic benefit consumption.
