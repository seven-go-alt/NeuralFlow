# Meridian Analytics — Security Exception and Risk Acceptance Procedure

**Document ID:** doc_compliance_exception
**Owner:** Security and Compliance
**Last updated:** 2026-03-05

## Purpose

This document defines the procedure for requesting a security exception when a standard compliance control cannot be implemented at Meridian Analytics. Deviations must be formally documented and approved at the appropriate authority level.

## Scope

This procedure applies to all Meridian Analytics systems, applications, processes, and third-party integrations where a standard compliance control cannot be fully implemented. It covers exceptions to controls defined in the Meridian Security Baseline, the SOC 2 control framework, the GDPR compliance checklist, and any other regulatory or contractual security requirements that Meridian is subject to.

## Procedure for requesting a security exception

The following procedure must be followed when a standard compliance control cannot be implemented and a security exception is needed:

1. **Identify the control gap:** The requestor identifies the specific compliance control that cannot be implemented and documents the technical, operational, or financial reason why implementation is not feasible. The requestor must also identify any compensating controls that are already in place or could be implemented to mitigate the risk.

2. **Submit a security exception request:** The requestor completes the Security Exception Request Form in the Compliance Portal. The form requires a description of the control, the reason for the exception, the affected systems or data, the proposed compensating controls, and a proposed duration for the exception. Submissions are automatically routed to the Security and Compliance team.

3. **Risk assessment:** The Security and Compliance team performs a formal risk assessment, evaluating the likelihood and impact of a security incident resulting from the exception. The assessment assigns a risk score using the Meridian Risk Matrix, which categorizes risk as Low, Medium, High, or Critical. The risk acceptance criteria are:
   - Low risk: Approved by the Security and Compliance Manager
   - Medium risk: Approved by the CISO
   - High risk: Approved by the CISO and the Chief Legal Officer
   - Critical risk: Not eligible for exception; the control must be implemented

4. **Remediation plan:** The requestor must include a remediation plan with a target date for implementing the standard compliance control. Security exceptions are granted for a maximum of 12 months. Extensions require submission of a new request with updated justification at least 30 days before the current exception expires.

5. **Approval and documentation:** Once approved, the exception is documented in the Compliance Portal with the approval details, risk score, and expiration date. The exception register is reviewed monthly by the Security and Compliance team.

6. **Monitoring and renewal:** Approved exceptions are monitored by the Security and Compliance team. Quarterly reviews assess whether the risk profile has changed. If the risk becomes Critical, the exception is immediately revoked and the control must be implemented.

## Risk acceptance register

All approved security exceptions are recorded in the Risk Acceptance Register maintained by the Security and Compliance team. The register includes the exception ID, requesting department, control description, risk score, approval date, expiration date, and current status. As of March 2026, there are 14 active security exceptions in the register, with an average risk score of Low.

## Exceptions to the exception procedure

Emergency exceptions may be granted verbally by the CISO in situations where an immediate threat requires bypassing a standard control. Verbal exceptions must be documented in writing within 48 hours and follow the standard submission process within 5 business days.

This policy was last updated on 5 March 2026. It will be reviewed semi-annually or after any significant security incident involving a risk acceptance.
