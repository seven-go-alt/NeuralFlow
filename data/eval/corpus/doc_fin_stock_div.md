# Meridian Analytics — Accounting Memo: Stock Dividend Accounting Under US GAAP

**Document ID:** doc_fin_stock_div
**Owner:** Finance — Accounting
**Last updated:** 2026-03-15

## Purpose

This memorandum documents the proper accounting treatment for a stock dividend declared at 15% of outstanding shares under US GAAP. Meridian Analytics maintains this guidance to ensure consistent application of ASC 505-20 (Distributions to Shareholders with Stock or Stock Rights) across all dividend declarations.

## Classification of stock dividend size

Under US GAAP, the accounting treatment for a stock dividend depends on its size relative to the outstanding shares. The prevailing guidance distinguishes between small stock dividends (generally less than 20–25% of outstanding shares) and large stock dividends (25% or more). A stock dividend of 15% of outstanding shares falls within the small stock dividend category, which requires capitalization of retained earnings at the fair value of the shares issued.

## Small stock dividend accounting

When Meridian declares a stock dividend of 15% of outstanding shares, the following accounting treatment applies under US GAAP. The dividend is classified as a small stock dividend because 15% is below the 20–25% threshold. The journal entry capitalizes retained earnings at the fair value of the additional shares distributed:

- **Dr. Retained Earnings** (at fair value per share multiplied by number of shares issued)
- **Cr. Common Stock** (at par value per share multiplied by number of shares issued)
- **Cr. Additional Paid-In Capital** (for the excess of fair value over par value)

The fair value used for this purpose is determined by the most recent independent valuation performed by the Board of Directors. Totals for retained earnings transferred are recorded at the aggregate fair value of the shares distributed.

## Effect on shareholders' equity

The stock dividend does not change total shareholders' equity; it reclassifies amounts within equity. Retained earnings decrease by the fair value of the shares issued, while common stock and additional paid-in capital increase by corresponding amounts. The number of outstanding shares increases proportionally, reducing the book value per share but leaving each shareholder's proportional ownership unchanged. Par value per share remains unaffected.

## Disclosure requirements

The financial statements must disclose the following for any stock dividend declared: the number of shares issued, the per-share fair value used for capitalization, the amount transferred from retained earnings, and the effect on earnings per share. These disclosures appear in the notes to the financial statements for the period in which the dividend is declared and distributed.
