# Revenue Recognition for Multi-Year Software Licensing Agreements under ASC 606

**Document ID:** doc_fin_software_rev.md
**Owner:** Revenue Accounting Department
**Last updated:** July 14, 2026

## Overview of ASC 606

ASC 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers) establishes a comprehensive five-step model for revenue recognition applicable to all industries, including software companies. The five steps are: (1) identify the contract with the customer, (2) identify the performance obligations in the contract, (3) determine the transaction price, (4) allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations, and (5) recognize revenue when or as each performance obligation is satisfied. For software companies engaged in multi-year licensing arrangements, the application of these steps requires careful analysis of the nature of the licenses and services provided.

## Identifying Performance Obligations

In a **multi-year licensing** agreement that includes both **perpetual licenses** and annual **maintenance fees**, the software company must first identify the distinct performance obligations. A perpetual license typically conveys the right to use the software as it exists at the point in time of delivery and is considered a distinct performance obligation. Maintenance fees, which generally include technical support, bug fixes, and unspecified software updates, are also a distinct performance obligation satisfied over time. These are separate promises within the contract and must be accounted for as distinct performance obligations under ASC 606.

## Revenue Recognition Pattern

**Perpetual licenses** are recognized at a **point in time** when control of the license transfers to the customer, which typically occurs at the delivery of the software. This timing reflects that the customer can direct the use of and obtain substantially all of the benefits from the software license immediately upon delivery. **Maintenance fees** are recognized **ratably** over the contract term, as the software company provides support and updates throughout the period. Under **ASC 606**, this ratable recognition reflects the fact that the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits of the maintenance services as they are provided.

## Allocation of Transaction Price

The transaction price, which is the total consideration the software company expects to receive, must be allocated to each performance obligation based on the relative standalone selling prices. The standalone selling price is the price at which the company would sell the license or maintenance services separately. Observable evidence of standalone selling prices is preferred, but when not available, the company may estimate these prices using an adjusted market assessment approach, expected cost plus margin approach, or residual approach if certain conditions are met. This allocation is critical because it determines the amount of revenue recognized at different points in time.

## Disclosures and Practical Considerations

ASC 606 requires extensive disclosures about **revenue recognition** policies, including the nature, amount, timing, and uncertainty of revenue and cash flows from contracts with customers. For multi-year licensing arrangements, disclosures should include the significant payment terms, the company's method of determining standalone selling prices, and the aggregate amount of the transaction price allocated to performance obligations that are unsatisfied as of the reporting date. Software companies should maintain detailed documentation of their revenue recognition policies and the application of the five-step model to ensure consistent application and audit readiness.
