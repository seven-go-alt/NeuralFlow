# Journal Entry for Accounts Receivable Write-Off Under the Allowance Method

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-002
**Owner:** Accounts Receivable Accounting Group
**Last Updated:** July 16, 2026

## Purpose

This memorandum documents the correct journal entry for writing off a customer accounts receivable balance under the allowance method, in accordance with US GAAP (ASC 310-10). The scenario addressed is the write-off of a $50,000 uncollectible receivable from a client that has filed for bankruptcy.

## Background

Meridian Analytics maintains accounts receivable on its balance sheet at net realizable value. Under the allowance method, an estimated allowance for doubtful accounts is established through a bad debt expense accrual at the end of each reporting period. When a specific account is subsequently determined to be uncollectible, the balance is written off against the allowance rather than directly against revenue or earnings.

The allowance method is required under US GAAP and provides a more accurate matching of bad debt expense with the revenues to which it relates. Meridian estimates its allowance using a combination of the aging method and specific identification for known high-risk accounts.

## Correct Journal Entry

When Meridian determines that a $50,000 accounts receivable is uncollectible and should be written off, the following journal entry is recorded:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Allowance for Doubtful Accounts | $50,000 | |
| Accounts Receivable | | $50,000 |

**Memo:** Write-off of uncollectible balance — Client ABC Corp., Chapter 7 bankruptcy filing

## Explanation of the Entry

The debit to **Allowance for Doubtful Accounts** reduces the contra-asset balance that was previously established through the bad debt expense accrual. The credit to **Accounts Receivable** removes the specific receivable from the subsidiary ledger and the general ledger control account.

Important observations about this entry:

- **No impact on net income:** The write-off entry does not affect the income statement because the bad debt expense was recognized in a prior period when the allowance was established.
- **No impact on net receivables:** The write-off reduces both gross accounts receivable and the allowance by the same amount, leaving net receivables unchanged.
- **No impact on total assets:** The reduction in gross receivables is offset by the reduction in the contra-asset account, so total assets remain unchanged.

## Recovery Considerations

If the written-off receivable is subsequently collected (e.g., partial payment from the bankruptcy trustee), Meridian would reverse the write-off by reinstating the receivable and the allowance, then recording the cash collection. This ensures that the allowance account continues to reflect management's best estimate of uncollectible amounts.

## Internal Control Requirements

All write-offs exceeding $10,000 require written approval from both the Credit Manager and the Controller. The $50,000 write-off documented herein was approved in accordance with this policy on July 14, 2026.
