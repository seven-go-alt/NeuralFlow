# Accounting Memorandum: Treatment of Start-Up Costs Under US GAAP and Internal Revenue Code Section 195

**Document ID:** doc_fin_startup
**Owner:** Office of the Corporate Controller, Meridian Analytics
**Last Updated:** March 10, 2026

## Overview of Start-Up Costs

Start-up costs include those incurred to create a new business or new business division, such as costs for advertising, employee training, consulting, legal and professional fees, and other pre-opening expenses. These costs arise before the commencement of active operations and are intended to enable the entity to begin generating revenue. The accounting treatment of start-up costs under US GAAP differs significantly from the tax treatment under Section 195 of the Internal Revenue Code. This memorandum summarizes both treatments for a new business division established by Meridian Analytics.

## US GAAP Treatment Under ASC 720

Under US GAAP, the guidance for start-up costs is found in ASC 720-15, which requires that start-up costs, including organization costs, be expensed as incurred. ASC 720-15 defines start-up costs broadly to include one-time activities related to opening a new facility, introducing a new product or service, conducting business in a new territory, or initiating a new process in an existing facility. The standard explicitly prohibits capitalization of start-up costs, even if the entity expects to generate future economic benefits from the activities. The rationale is that the future benefits are too uncertain and not reliably measurable at the time the costs are incurred. Therefore, Meridian Analytics must expense all start-up costs associated with its new business division in the period incurred for financial reporting purposes.

## Tax Treatment Under Section 195

For tax purposes, Section 195 of the Internal Revenue Code provides a different treatment. Section 195 allows taxpayers to elect to amortize start-up expenditures over a period of not less than 180 months, beginning with the month in which the active trade or business begins. Alternatively, a taxpayer may elect to deduct up to $5,000 of start-up costs in the year the trade or business begins, with the $5,000 amount reduced dollar-for-dollar by the amount by which total start-up costs exceed $50,000. Any start-up costs not deductible in the first year must be amortized over the 180-month period. Start-up costs for tax purposes include those incurred before the day the active trade or business begins, such as advertising, employee training, and other pre-business expenses.

## Comparison of GAAP and Tax Treatment

The key difference between US GAAP and tax treatment lies in the timing of expense recognition. Under US GAAP, start-up costs are expensed immediately as incurred, resulting in an immediate reduction to net income. Under Section 195, start-up costs may be capitalized and amortized over 180 months, or immediately deducted up to the $5,000 threshold if elected. The GAAP treatment is generally more conservative, recognizing expense earlier than the tax treatment. This difference creates a temporary book-tax difference that must be tracked through deferred tax accounting, as the start-up costs will be recognized in different periods for book versus tax purposes.

## Financial Reporting Implications

Meridian Analytics should record a deferred tax asset for the temporary difference arising from the different treatment of start-up costs under GAAP and tax. The deferred tax asset represents the future tax benefit from amortization over the 180-month period. A valuation allowance should be assessed if it is more likely than not that some portion of the deferred tax asset will not be realized. The entity should maintain detailed schedules of start-up costs to support both the GAAP expense recognition and the tax amortization computations.
