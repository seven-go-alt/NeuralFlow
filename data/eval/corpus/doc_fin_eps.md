# Diluted EPS Calculation Using the Treasury Stock Method for Stock Options

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-010
**Owner:** Earnings Per Share Reporting Team
**Last Updated:** July 15, 2026

## Purpose

This memorandum documents the process for calculating diluted earnings per share (EPS) when Meridian Analytics has outstanding stock options with an exercise price below the average market price of its common stock. As a privately held company, Meridian uses its most recent valuation price as a proxy for market price in these calculations.

## Applicable Guidance

Under ASC 260-10-45 (Earnings Per Share), entities with complex capital structures must present both basic and diluted EPS. Diluted EPS reflects the potential dilution that could occur if dilutive securities, including stock options, were exercised or converted into common stock. The treasury stock method is the prescribed approach for calculating the dilutive effect of stock options.

## The Treasury Stock Method

The treasury stock method assumes that the proceeds from the exercise of stock options are used by the entity to repurchase shares of its own common stock at the average market price during the period. The incremental shares (the difference between shares issued upon exercise and shares repurchased) are added to the denominator in the diluted EPS calculation.

The steps are as follows:

1. **Determine proceeds from exercise:** Multiply the number of options by the exercise price per share
2. **Calculate shares repurchased:** Divide the total proceeds by the average market price per share
3. **Compute incremental shares:** Subtract the shares repurchased from the shares issued upon exercise
4. **Add incremental shares to denominator:** Include the incremental shares in the diluted EPS denominator

## Illustration

Assume Meridian has 100,000 stock options outstanding with an exercise price of $15 per share. The average market price for Meridian's common stock during the period is $25 per share.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|------|------------|--------|
| Proceeds from exercise | 100,000 x $15 | $1,500,000 |
| Shares repurchased | $1,500,000 / $25 | 60,000 |
| Shares issued upon exercise | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| Incremental shares | 100,000 - 60,000 | 40,000 |

The 40,000 incremental shares are added to the weighted-average shares outstanding in the diluted EPS denominator.

## Anti-Dilution Considerations

Options are dilutive only when the exercise price is below the average market price. If the exercise price exceeds the average market price, the options are anti-dilutive and are excluded from the diluted EPS calculation because including them would increase rather than decrease EPS.

In the illustration above, the exercise price of $15 is below the average market price of $25, so the options are dilutive. If the exercise price were $30 (above the $25 market price), the options would be anti-dilutive and excluded.

## Time-Weighting Considerations

Options that are outstanding for only part of the period are time-weighted in the diluted EPS calculation. For example, if 50,000 options were granted on July 1 and 50,000 were outstanding for the full year, the incremental shares would be calculated separately for each tranche based on the portion of the year they were outstanding.

## Conclusion

Meridian Analytics calculates diluted EPS for stock options using the treasury stock method, assuming proceeds from exercise are used to repurchase shares at the average market price. Only options with an exercise price below the average market price are included in the diluted EPS calculation.
