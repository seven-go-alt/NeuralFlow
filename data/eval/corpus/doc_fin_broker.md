# Accounting Memorandum: Broker-Dealer Net Capital Requirements Under SEC Rule 15c3-1

## Overview of SEC Rule 15c3-1

SEC Rule 15c3-1, commonly known as the Net Capital Rule, establishes minimum capital requirements for broker-dealers registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The rule is designed to ensure that broker-dealers maintain sufficient liquid assets to meet their obligations to customers and creditors. The rule underwent significant amendments effective in 2023, which updated the net capital computation methodology and revised certain requirements applicable to broker-dealers operating in the current market environment. This memorandum outlines the minimum capital requirements for a general-purpose broker-dealer.

## Minimum Net Capital Requirements

Under SEC Rule 15c3-1, a broker-dealer must maintain minimum net capital equal to the greater of a fixed dollar amount or a percentage-based requirement. For most full-service broker-dealers that carry customer accounts, the minimum net capital is $250,000. For broker-dealers that introduce transactions and accounts to another clearing broker-dealer on a fully disclosed basis and do not handle customer funds or securities, the minimum net capital requirement is generally $100,000. Certain limited-purpose broker-dealers, such as those engaged exclusively in the sale of variable annuity products or mutual fund shares, may qualify for a reduced minimum of $100,000 or less under specific conditions.

## Aggregate Debit Items Computation

In addition to the fixed dollar minimum, a broker-dealer that carries customer accounts must maintain net capital of at least 2 percent of aggregate debit items as computed under the customer reserve formula in SEC Rule 15c3-3. Aggregate debit items represent the total amount of customer-related receivables, including margin loans and other amounts due from customers. As customer debit balances increase, the net capital requirement also increases proportionally, ensuring that the broker-dealer maintains capital commensurate with its customer financing activities.

## The 2023 Net Capital Rule Updates

The 2023 amendments to SEC Rule 15c3-1 introduced several important changes to the net capital computation methodology. These updates included modifications to the haircut percentages applied to certain securities positions held by broker-dealers, revisions to the treatment of reverse repurchase agreements, and updated capital charges for derivatives and other complex financial instruments. The amendments also clarified the treatment of certain types of subordinated debt and modified the deduction requirements for unsecured receivables. Broker-dealers must ensure their net capital computations comply with the updated methodology as of the effective date of the amendments.

## Ongoing Compliance and Reporting

Broker-dealers are required to compute net capital on a weekly or monthly basis depending on their business activities and the amount of capital they maintain relative to the minimum requirement. Net capital computations must be filed with the SEC and designated examining authorities as part of the broker-dealer's periodic financial reports, including the FOCUS report filings. Failure to maintain required net capital levels can result in immediate regulatory action, including restrictions on business activities, fines, or suspension of registration.
