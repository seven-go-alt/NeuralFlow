# Goodwill Impairment Testing Under IFRS 3 versus US GAAP ASC 350

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-008
**Owner:** International Accounting Standards Group
**Last Updated:** July 15, 2026

## Purpose

This memorandum compares the treatment of goodwill impairment under IFRS 3 (Business Combinations) and US GAAP ASC 350 (Intangibles — Goodwill and Other). Meridian Analytics reports under US GAAP but has foreign subsidiaries that report under IFRS, making understanding of both standards essential for consolidation purposes.

## Recognition and Initial Measurement

Under both IFRS 3 and ASC 350, goodwill is recognized as the excess of the purchase consideration over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired in a business combination. Both standards require goodwill to be allocated to cash-generating units (CGUs) under IFRS or reporting units under US GAAP. The initial recognition and measurement of goodwill are substantially converged between the two frameworks.

## IFRS 3 Impairment Approach

Under IFRS 3 and IAS 36, goodwill impairment testing follows a **one-step test**:

1. The carrying amount of the CGU (including allocated goodwill) is compared to its recoverable amount
2. The recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use
3. If the carrying amount exceeds the recoverable amount, an impairment loss is recognized
4. Impairment losses are allocated first to goodwill, then to other assets on a pro rata basis

Impairment testing is required **annually** and whenever there is an indication of potential impairment. IFRS does not permit a qualitative assessment as a screening mechanism.

## US GAAP ASC 350 Approach

Under ASC 350, goodwill impairment testing can involve a **qualitative assessment** before performing a quantitative test:

1. **Qualitative assessment (optional):** An entity may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount
2. **If the qualitative assessment indicates impairment is not more likely than not, no further testing is required**
3. **One-step quantitative test (required if qualitative assessment is not performed or indicates potential impairment):** The carrying amount of the reporting unit is compared to its fair value. If the carrying amount exceeds fair value, an impairment loss is recognized for the excess, limited to the goodwill allocated to that reporting unit

ASC 350 eliminated the prior two-step test (which required computing implied goodwill) in favor of a simpler one-step approach. However, the option to perform a qualitative assessment remains a key difference from IFRS.

## Key Differences Summary

| Aspect | IFRS 3 / IAS 36 | US GAAP ASC 350 |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| Qualitative assessment | Not permitted | Optional screening step |
| Test level | Cash-generating unit | Reporting unit |
| Recoverable amount | Higher of FV less costs to sell and VIU | Fair value |
| Impairment allocation | First to goodwill, then pro rata to other assets | Initially to goodwill only |
| Reversal | Required if conditions improve | Prohibited |

## Conclusion

Meridian applies IFRS 3 for its foreign subsidiaries. The key practical difference is that IFRS 3 requires annual impairment testing with a one-step comparison to recoverable amount without a qualitative screening option, whereas ASC 350 allows Meridian to first perform a qualitative assessment to determine whether the one-step quantitative test is necessary.
