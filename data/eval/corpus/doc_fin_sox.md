# Material Weakness Reporting Thresholds Under SOX Section 404

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-011
**Owner:** Internal Audit and SOX Compliance Group
**Last Updated:** July 16, 2026

## Regulatory Framework

This memorandum documents the thresholds for identifying and reporting a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting under Section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX). Meridian Analytics is subject to SOX Section 404 requirements as a reporting company with public debt securities registered with the SEC under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

## Definitions Under PCAOB Standards

The Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB), through Auditing Standard No. 2201, establishes the framework for evaluating internal control deficiencies. Deficiencies are categorized into three severity levels:

- **Control deficiency:** A deficiency in the design or operation of a control that does not rise to the level of a significant deficiency or material weakness.
- **Significant deficiency:** A deficiency, or a combination of deficiencies, that is less severe than a material weakness yet important enough to merit attention by those responsible for oversight of financial reporting.
- **Material weakness:** A deficiency, or a combination of deficiencies, in internal control over financial reporting such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of the annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis.

## Material Weakness Definition

Under PCAOB standards, a material weakness exists when both of the following conditions are present:

1. **Reasonable possibility:** There is a reasonable possibility that a misstatement could occur. The PCAOB defines "reasonable possibility" as "the likelihood of an event is either reasonably possible or probable" (consistent with the contingency framework in ASC 450-20), which is generally interpreted as a likelihood greater than remote but less than probable.

2. **Material misstatement:** The potential misstatement would be material to the financial statements. Materiality is assessed based on both quantitative and qualitative factors, with quantitative thresholds typically beginning at 5% of pre-tax income for most companies.

## Assessment Process

Meridian follows a structured process for evaluating control deficiencies:

1. **Identify the deficiency:** Through testing of design and operating effectiveness
2. **Evaluate likelihood:** Assess the probability that a misstatement could result
3. **Evaluate magnitude:** Estimate the potential misstatement amount
4. **Consider compensating controls:** Determine whether other controls mitigate the deficiency
5. **Aggregate similar deficiencies:** Combine deficiencies that affect the same account or assertion
6. **Conclude on severity:** Determine whether the deficiency constitutes a material weakness, significant deficiency, or control deficiency

## Disclosure Implications

If a material weakness is identified and not remediated by the end of the fiscal year, Meridian must:

- Disclose the material weakness in the annual 10-K filing under Item 9A (Controls and Procedures)
- Provide a detailed description of the nature of the material weakness and its impact on financial reporting
- Describe management's remediation plan, including timelines and status
- Receive an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting from the independent auditor

## Conclusion

A material weakness under SOX Section 404 is a deficiency where there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of the financial statements will not be prevented or detected. PCAOB standards define the severity levels, and Meridian evaluates all identified deficiencies through this framework to ensure accurate reporting.
