# Classification and Measurement of Financial Assets under IFRS 9

**Document ID:** doc_fin_fin_asset.md
**Owner:** Financial Instruments Accounting Group
**Last updated:** July 13, 2026

## Overview of IFRS 9 Classification Model

IFRS 9 (Financial Instruments) replaced the classification and measurement requirements of IAS 39 and introduced a fundamentally different approach for categorizing financial assets. The standard establishes a classification model based on the entity's business model for managing financial assets and the contractual cash flow characteristics of the assets. Under IFRS 9, the classification determines the measurement basis and the recognition of gains and losses, directly affecting **balance sheet valuation**. Meridian Analytics, in preparing its IFRS-compliant financial statements, applies these classification requirements to its portfolio of financial assets.

## Classification Categories under IFRS 9

The previous IAS 39 categories of **held-to-maturity**, available-for-sale, and loans and receivables have been replaced under IFRS 9. The new categories are **amortized cost**, Fair Value Through Other Comprehensive Income (FVTOCI), and Fair Value Through Profit or Loss (FVTPL). Debt instruments are classified based on both the business model test and the contractual cash flow characteristics test. Equity investments are generally measured at FVTPL, with an irrevocable election available to present fair value changes in other comprehensive income.

## Amortized Cost for Debt Instruments

A debt instrument is measured at **amortized cost** under **IFRS 9** if both conditions are met: the asset is held within a business model whose objective is to hold assets to collect contractual cash flows, and the contractual terms give rise to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest (the SPPI test). This category replaces the former held-to-maturity classification under IAS 39. Instruments classified as amortized cost are initially measured at fair value and subsequently measured at amortized cost using the effective interest method, with impairment under the expected credit loss model.

## FVTOCI Classification for Debt Instruments

Debt instruments that meet the SPPI test but are held within a business model whose objective is achieved by both collecting contractual cash flows and selling financial assets are classified as FVTOCI. This category is the IFRS 9 replacement for the former **available-for-sale** classification under IAS 39. Under FVTOCI, interest income calculated using the effective interest method, foreign exchange gains and losses, and impairment gains and losses are recognized in profit or loss, while other fair value changes are recognized in other comprehensive income. Upon derecognition, the cumulative fair value changes previously recognized in other comprehensive income are reclassified to profit or loss.

## Equity Instruments and the FVTOCI Election

Equity instruments are measured at FVTPL under IFRS 9. However, the standard permits an irrevocable election at initial recognition to present subsequent fair value changes in other comprehensive income (FVTOCI for equity instruments). This election is made on an instrument-by-instrument basis and, once made, cannot be changed. Under this election, dividend income is recognized in profit or loss, but gains and losses on disposal are not reclassified to profit or loss. This treatment differs significantly from the IAS 39 available-for-sale classification, where gains and losses on equity investments were reclassified to profit or loss upon disposal.

## Disclosures and Transition

IFRS 9 requires extensive disclosures about the classification and measurement of financial assets, including the carrying amounts of financial assets measured at amortized cost, FVTOCI, and FVTPL, as well as the bases for determining fair values. Reclassifications between categories are permitted only when the entity's business model for managing the financial assets changes, and such reclassifications are applied prospectively. Entities that previously applied IAS 39 were required to provide extensive transition disclosures when adopting IFRS 9.
