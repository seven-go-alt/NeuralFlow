# Meridian Analytics — Accounting Memo: Inventory Measurement at Lower of Cost or Market

**Document ID:** doc_fin_inventory
**Owner:** Finance — Accounting
**Last updated:** 2026-03-10

## Purpose

This memorandum documents the proper methodology for measuring inventory at the lower of cost or net realizable value under US GAAP (also known as lower of cost or market or LCM), specifically addressing scenarios where replacement cost has declined significantly below original purchase price. The guidance applies to Meridian Analytics' hardware inventory held for resale to enterprise customers.

## Lower of cost or market framework

Under US GAAP (ASC 330, Inventory), inventory is measured at the lower of cost or market. The term "market" is defined as the current replacement cost, subject to two constraints: market shall not exceed the net realizable value (ceiling) and shall not be less than net realizable value reduced by a normal profit margin (floor). When replacement cost has fallen below the original purchase price, an entity must determine whether a write-down is required by applying the LCM framework.

## Scenario: Replacement cost decline of 20%

Meridian holds an inventory item with an original purchase price of $1,000 per unit. The replacement cost has declined by 20%, to $800 per unit. Under the LCM rule, the following analysis is performed:

1. **Cost**: $1,000 (original purchase price)
2. **Replacement cost**: $800 (declined 20%)
3. **Net realizable value (NRV)**: Estimated selling price less costs of completion and disposal. Assume NRV is $950 per unit.
4. **NRV less normal profit margin**: Assume $850.
5. **Market (designated market value)**: The middle value of replacement cost ($800), NRV ($950), and NRV less normal profit ($850). The designated market value is $850, because replacement cost ($800) falls below the floor ($850), so the floor becomes the market value.
6. **LCM**: Lower of cost ($1,000) and market ($850) = $850.

A write-down of $150 per unit ($1,000 − $850) is required to reduce inventory to the lower of cost or market. This write-down is recognized in cost of goods sold in the period of decline.

## Measuring net realizable value

For Meridian's inventory, net realizable value is estimated based on the expected selling price to enterprise customers, less any estimated costs of completion, shipping, and selling expenses. The company reviews NRV estimates quarterly and adjusts write-downs accordingly. If replacement cost continues to decline, the write-down is increased proportionally. If replacement cost subsequently recovers, US GAAP does not permit the reversal of write-downs.

## Journal entry for write-down

The journal entry to record the LCM write-down is:

- **Dr. Cost of Goods Sold** (write-down amount)
- **Cr. Inventory** (write-down amount)

For Meridian's scenario, the write-down of $150 per unit is recorded directly against the inventory account. The reduced carrying amount becomes the new cost basis for that inventory item and is not subsequently written up.

## Disclosure

The financial statements must disclose the basis of inventory valuation (LCM) and the amount of any write-downs recognized during the period. Meridian includes these disclosures in the notes to the financial statements.
