# Maximum Deductible IRA Contribution for a 45-Year-Old Single Filer in Tax Year 2026

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-007
**Owner:** Employee Benefits and Retirement Team
**Last Updated:** July 16, 2026

## Purpose

This memorandum provides guidance on the maximum deductible Individual Retirement Account (IRA) contribution for a 45-year-old employee of Meridian Analytics who is covered by the company's workplace retirement plan (a 401(k) plan). The guidance applies to tax year 2026.

## Background

Meridian Analytics sponsors a 401(k) retirement plan for its employees. Employees participating in the 401(k) plan are considered "active participants" in a workplace retirement plan for IRA deduction purposes. This status triggers income-based phase-out limits on the deductibility of traditional IRA contributions under IRC Section 219(g).

A 45-year-old employee is under age 50 and therefore is not eligible for the catch-up contribution provision that allows individuals aged 50 and older to contribute an additional $1,000 per year to their IRA.

## Maximum Contribution Limit

For tax year 2026, the maximum annual IRA contribution limit is **$7,000** for individuals under age 50. The 45-year-old employee's maximum contribution is $7,000, subject to the deduction phase-out described below.

## Deduction Phase-Out for Single Filers

For a single filer who is an active participant in a workplace retirement plan in 2026, the deductible portion of the IRA contribution phases out based on modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) as follows:

| MAGI Range | Deduction Status |
|------------|------------------|
| Less than $79,000 | Full deduction allowed |
| $79,000 to $89,000 | Partial deduction (phased out proportionally) |
| More than $89,000 | No deduction allowed |

The phase-out range for single filers is $79,000 to $89,000 of MAGI. Within this range, the deductible amount is reduced proportionally based on where the employee's MAGI falls within the range.

## Calculation Example

If the 45-year-old single filer has a MAGI of $84,000 in 2026:

1. The MAGI exceeds the $79,000 phase-out floor by $5,000
2. The phase-out range is $10,000 ($89,000 - $79,000)
3. The deductible portion is reduced by $5,000 / $10,000 = 50%
4. If the employee contributes the full $7,000, the deductible amount is $3,500 (50% of $7,000)

## Roth IRA Alternative

Employees who are ineligible for deductible traditional IRA contributions due to the phase-out limits may consider contributing to a Roth IRA, subject to separate income limits. Roth IRA contributions are nondeductible but qualified distributions are tax-free.

## Conclusion

For a 45-year-old single filer covered by a workplace retirement plan in 2026, the maximum deductible IRA contribution is $7,000, but the deductibility phases out between $79,000 and $89,000 of MAGI. The employee is not eligible for the $1,000 catch-up contribution.
