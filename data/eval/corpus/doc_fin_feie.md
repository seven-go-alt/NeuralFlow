# Foreign Earned Income Exclusion for US Taxpayers Abroad in 2026

**Document ID:** doc_fin_feie.md
**Owner:** International Tax Services
**Last updated:** July 8, 2026

## Overview of the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion

The **Foreign Earned Income Exclusion** (FEIE), codified in Section 911 of the Internal Revenue Code, allows qualified US citizens and resident aliens living abroad to exclude a portion of their **foreign earned income** from **US taxation**. To qualify for the exclusion, the taxpayer must have a tax home in a foreign country and meet either the bona fide residence test or the physical presence test. The FEIE is designed to prevent double taxation of income earned by US expatriates and to provide tax relief for Americans living and working in foreign jurisdictions.

## Maximum Exclusion Amount for 2026

For tax year **2026**, the FEIE maximum **exclusion** is **$130,000** (this figure reflects the inflation-indexed amount under IRC Section 911). The exclusion amount is adjusted annually for inflation, and the precise figure is published by the IRS in the applicable revenue procedure. The indexed amount has increased steadily over the years; for context, the inflation-adjusted figure was $120,000 in 2023, $126,500 in 2024 and 2025, and the 2026 amount reflects the continued indexing mechanism. Taxpayers must verify the exact published figure when filing.

## Qualifying for the Exclusion

To claim the FEIE, the taxpayer must first establish that their tax home is in a foreign country. The tax home is generally the taxpayer's regular or principal place of business. If the taxpayer maintains a home in the United States while working abroad, the tax home analysis requires careful scrutiny of the facts and circumstances. Additionally, the taxpayer must satisfy one of two tests. The bona fide residence test requires the taxpayer to be a bona fide resident of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year. The physical presence test requires the taxpayer to be physically present in a foreign country for at least 330 full days during any 12-consecutive-month period.

## Phase-Out and Interaction with Other Provisions

The FEIE phases out after a certain income threshold. Once the taxpayer's total foreign earned income exceeds the applicable amount, the exclusion is reduced or eliminated. Specifically, the exclusion phases out once the taxpayer's income exceeds the threshold amount, and it is completely eliminated at higher income levels. Taxpayers who claim the FEIE cannot also claim foreign tax credits for the same income that is excluded. However, foreign tax credits may still be available for foreign income that exceeds the exclusion amount. The relationship between the FEIE and foreign tax credits requires careful planning to maximize the overall tax benefit.

## Reporting Requirements

Taxpayers claiming the FEIE must file Form 2555 (Foreign Earned Income Exclusion) with their annual US tax return. The form requires detailed information about the taxpayer's foreign residence, employer, nature of work, and days of physical presence. Supporting documentation, including employment contracts, pay records, and evidence of foreign residence, should be maintained. Failure to properly document eligibility for the FEIE can result in the disallowance of the exclusion and the imposition of penalties and interest on underpaid tax.
