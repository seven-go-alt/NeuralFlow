# Equity Method Accounting for Investments under ASC 323

**Document ID:** doc_fin_equity.md
**Owner:** Financial Reporting Department
**Last updated:** July 10, 2026

## Overview of the Equity Method

The equity method of accounting, governed by ASC 323 (Investments -- Equity Method and Joint Ventures), is used when an investor has the ability to exercise significant influence over the operating and financial policies of an investee. Under this method, the investment is initially recorded at cost and subsequently adjusted for the investor's proportionate share of the investee's earnings or losses, dividends received, and other changes in the investee's equity.

## Ownership Percentage Threshold

ASC 323 establishes a bright-line threshold regarding the **ownership percentage** that creates a presumption of **significant influence**. An investor that holds **20% ownership or more** of an investee's voting stock is presumed to have significant influence, making application of the **equity method** mandatory under **ASC 323**. This presumption applies unless there is clear evidence to the contrary that the investor does not have significant influence despite meeting the ownership threshold. Such contrary evidence may include the investee's opposition to the investor's participation in policy-making processes, inability to obtain representation on the board of directors, or the existence of a majority owner with control.

## Factors Indicating Significant Influence Below 20%

Ownership of less than 20% does not automatically preclude application of the equity method. ASC 323 identifies several qualitative factors that may indicate the existence of significant influence even at lower ownership percentages. These factors include representation on the investee's board of directors, participation in policy-making processes, material intercompany transactions, interchange of managerial personnel, and technological dependency. When these factors are present, the equity method may be appropriate even when the ownership percentage falls below the 20% threshold.

## Initial Recognition and Subsequent Measurement

Upon initial recognition, an investment qualifying for the equity method is recorded at cost. In subsequent periods, the carrying amount of the investment is increased or decreased by the investor's proportionate share of the investee's net income or loss and decreased by dividends received from the investee. The investor also recognizes its proportionate share of other comprehensive income items of the investee. If the investor's share of losses exceeds the carrying amount of the investment, the equity method generally ceases to be applied, and losses are not recognized unless the investor has guaranteed obligations of the investee or is otherwise committed to provide further financial support.

## Disclosure Requirements

Entities applying the equity method must provide certain disclosures in their financial statements. These include the name and description of each equity method investee, the ownership percentage held, the carrying amount of the investment, and the investor's share of the investee's earnings or losses. Additionally, summarized financial information of equity method investees, including total assets, liabilities, revenues, and net income, is typically required to be disclosed. These disclosures enable users of financial statements to understand the nature and financial effects of the entity's investments accounted for under the equity method.
