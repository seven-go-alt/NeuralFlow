# Accounting Memorandum: Amortization of Acquired Intangible Assets

**Document ID:** doc_fin_intangible
**Owner:** Office of the Corporate Controller, Meridian Analytics
**Last Updated:** February 10, 2026

## Overview of Intangible Asset Amortization

Under US GAAP, an acquired intangible asset with a determinable useful life must be amortized over its estimated useful life. When the intangible asset is a patent acquired from a third party, the amortization period should reflect the period over which the asset is expected to contribute to the cash flows of the entity. This memorandum addresses the appropriate amortization method and period for a patent acquired for $500,000 with a remaining legal life of 12 years but an expected economic life of only 8 years.

## Determining the Amortization Period

The amortization period for an acquired intangible asset is the shorter of its legal life and its expected economic life. The legal life of a patent is the remaining term of the patent protection granted by the US Patent and Trademark Office. However, the economic life may be shorter than the legal life due to factors such as technological obsolescence, changes in market demand, or the emergence of competing technologies. In this scenario, the remaining legal life is 12 years, but the expected economic life is only 8 years. Therefore, the amortization period is 8 years, being the shorter of the two. This approach ensures that the cost of the intangible asset is allocated to expense over the period during which the entity expects to derive economic benefits.

## Amortization Method

The straight-line method is the default and most commonly applied method for amortizing acquired intangible assets unless another systematic method more faithfully represents the pattern in which the economic benefits are consumed. Straight-line amortization yields an equal annual expense over the amortization period, which is appropriate when the benefits derived from the intangible asset are expected to be realized evenly over its useful life. No other method would be justified in this case absent specific evidence supporting an accelerated or decelerated pattern of benefit consumption.

## Calculation of Annual Amortization

The annual amortization expense is calculated by dividing the acquisition cost by the amortization period:

- Acquisition cost: $500,000
- Amortization period: 8 years
- Annual amortization expense: $500,000 divided by 8 equals $62,500

The entity should record an annual amortization entry debiting amortization expense and crediting accumulated amortization for $62,500 each year over the 8-year period. If the patent is retired or disposed of before the end of its amortization period, any remaining unamortized cost should be recognized as a loss on disposal.

## Impairment Considerations

Although the patent is being amortized over its economic life, the entity must also test the intangible asset for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that its carrying amount may not be recoverable. Such indicators may include a significant decline in the market value of the patent, adverse changes in the legal or regulatory environment, or a current-period operating loss combined with a history of losses. If impairment exists, the entity must write down the asset to its fair value and recognize an impairment loss in the income statement.
