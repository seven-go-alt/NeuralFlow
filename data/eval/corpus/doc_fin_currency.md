# Accounting Memorandum: Foreign Currency Translation Under ASC 830

**Document ID:** doc_fin_currency
**Owner:** Office of the Corporate Controller, Meridian Analytics
**Last Updated:** January 22, 2026

## Overview of ASC 830 Foreign Currency Matters

ASC 830, Foreign Currency Matters, establishes the accounting and reporting standards for foreign currency transactions and the translation of foreign currency financial statements. When a US parent company consolidates a foreign subsidiary whose functional currency is not the US dollar, the subsidiary's financial statements must be translated into the reporting currency of the parent. This memorandum addresses the treatment of foreign currency translation gains arising from the consolidation of a European subsidiary whose functional currency is the euro.

## Determining the Functional Currency

Under ASC 830, the functional currency of a foreign subsidiary is the currency of the primary economic environment in which the subsidiary operates. For a European subsidiary that conducts its operations, sales, and financing activities predominantly in euros, the euro is its functional currency. The subsidiary maintains its books and records in euros, and its financial statements are prepared in euros before translation into US dollars for consolidation. Once the functional currency is determined, it should be used consistently unless significant changes in economic facts and circumstances indicate that the functional currency has changed.

## Translation Method Under ASC 830

When a foreign subsidiary's functional currency is the euro (distinct from the parent's reporting currency, the US dollar), ASC 830 requires the current rate method for translation. Under this method, assets and liabilities are translated at the spot exchange rate in effect at the balance sheet date. Revenue and expense items are translated at the exchange rates in effect on the dates those items are recognized, or at appropriately weighted average rates for the period. Equity accounts are translated at historical exchange rates. The resulting translation adjustment reflects the effect of changes in exchange rates between the functional currency and the reporting currency.

## Reporting of Translation Gains and Losses

The critical question is where to report foreign currency translation gains and losses in the financial statements. Under ASC 830-30-45-12, translation adjustments arising from the consolidation of a foreign subsidiary with a functional currency different from the parent's reporting currency are reported in Other Comprehensive Income (OCI). They are accumulated as a separate component of stockholders' equity, commonly labeled as Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income (AOCI). These translation gains or losses are not recognized in net income unless there is a sale or complete liquidation of the foreign subsidiary.

## Distinction from Transaction Gains and Losses

It is important to distinguish foreign currency translation gains from foreign currency transaction gains and losses. Translation gains arise from the process of converting an entire set of financial statements from the functional currency to the reporting currency for consolidation purposes. By contrast, transaction gains and losses arise from the settlement of foreign currency-denominated receivables or payables. Foreign currency transaction gains and losses are included in net income in the period in which exchange rates change, consistent with ASC 830-20. Translation adjustments reported in OCI are not subject to reclassification into net income until the entity sells or substantially liquidates its investment in the foreign subsidiary.
