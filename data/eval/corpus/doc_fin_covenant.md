# Balance Sheet Classification of Loan Covenant Requiring a Minimum 1.5 Current Ratio

**Document ID:** MER-FIN-2026-009
**Owner:** Financial Reporting and Debt Compliance Group
**Last Updated:** July 16, 2026

## Issue

Meridian Analytics has outstanding debt subject to a loan covenant that requires the borrower to maintain a minimum current ratio of 1.5 at each reporting date. This memorandum addresses how the related debt should be classified on the balance sheet for financial reporting purposes under US GAAP (ASC 470-10).

## Background

The loan agreement with Meridian's primary lender includes affirmative financial covenants, one of which requires that the consolidated current ratio (current assets divided by current liabilities) remain at or above 1.5 at the end of each fiscal quarter. As of the most recent balance sheet date, Meridian's current ratio is 2.1, which is in compliance with the covenant.

## Applicable Guidance

Under ASC 470-10-45, the classification of debt as current or non-current depends on whether a covenant violation has occurred or whether it is probable that a violation will occur within the next year. Specifically:

- If the borrower is in compliance with all covenants and no violation has occurred, the debt is classified based on its original contractual maturity
- If a covenant violation has occurred that gives the lender the right to demand repayment within one year, the debt must be classified as current
- If a covenant violation has occurred but the lender has waived its right to demand repayment for a period exceeding one year, the debt may remain classified as non-current

## Classification Analysis for Meridian's Current Situation

Since Meridian is in compliance with the 1.5 current ratio covenant (actual ratio of 2.1 exceeds the 1.5 requirement), the debt is classified as non-current or current based on its contractual maturity date, not based on the covenant itself.

However, if a future reporting period shows a current ratio at or below 1.5, a violation would occur. In that scenario, the classification depends on whether the lender has the right to demand repayment:

1. **If violation gives lender demand rights within one year:** The debt must be reclassified as a current liability, even if the original maturity is longer than one year
2. **If waiver is obtained:** The debt remains non-current for the duration of the waiver period
3. **If cure period exists:** The borrower may be able to avoid reclassification if the cure period extends beyond one year and Meridian can remedy the violation within that period

## Disclosure Requirements

Regardless of classification, Meridian must disclose in the notes to the financial statements:

- The existence and terms of financial covenants
- The required minimum current ratio of 1.5
- Whether the entity is in compliance as of the reporting date
- Any covenant violations or events of default during the period

## Conclusion

When Meridian is in compliance with the 1.5 current ratio covenant, the debt is classified based on its contractual maturity. If a violation were to occur giving the lender the right to demand repayment within one year, the debt must be classified as a current liability.
