# Accounting Memorandum: Like-Kind Exchanges Under Section 1031 After the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act

**Document ID:** doc_fin_1031
**Owner:** Office of the Tax Controller, Meridian Analytics
**Last Updated:** February 28, 2026

## Overview of Section 1031 Like-Kind Exchanges

Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code allows a taxpayer to defer recognition of gain or loss on the exchange of business or investment property held for productive use if the property is exchanged for property of a like kind. Before the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), which took effect for exchanges completed after December 31, 2017, Section 1031 applied broadly to both real property and tangible personal property used in a trade or business or held for investment. The TCJA significantly narrowed the scope of Section 1031 by limiting its application exclusively to real property.

## Treatment of Real Property Exchanges After TCJA

For exchanges of real property completed after December 31, 2017, Section 1031 continues to provide for the deferral of gain or loss. Real property includes land, buildings, and inherently permanent structures, as well as structural components of those buildings and structures. A taxpayer exchanging qualifying real property for like-kind real property may defer recognition of gain realized on the exchange, provided the transaction satisfies all other requirements of Section 1031, including the identification and receipt timing rules. The basis of the replacement property is adjusted to reflect the deferred gain, and the holding period of the relinquished property carries over to the replacement property.

## Treatment of Personal Property After TCJA

The TCJA amended Section 1031 to expressly limit its application to exchanges of real property not held primarily for sale. Personal property exchanges, including exchanges of machinery, equipment, vehicles, aircraft, artwork, and other tangible personal property used in a trade or business, are no longer eligible for like-kind exchange treatment under Section 1031. A taxpayer who exchanges personal property for like-kind personal property after the TCJA effective date must recognize any realized gain or loss in the year of the exchange. This change represents a significant departure from prior law, under which many types of tangible personal property routinely qualified for tax-deferred exchange treatment.

## Mechanics of Deferred Gain Recognition

When a qualifying like-kind exchange of real property occurs, the taxpayer does not recognize gain or loss at the time of the exchange. Instead, the realized gain is deferred by reducing the tax basis of the replacement property. For example, if a taxpayer exchanges real property with a basis of $500,000 and a fair value of $800,000 for replacement real property worth $800,000, the realized gain of $300,000 is deferred. The taxpayer takes a basis of $500,000 in the replacement property. If the taxpayer receives boot, such as cash or non-like-kind property, gain is recognized to the extent of the lesser of the realized gain or the boot received.

## Planning Considerations

Taxpayers engaging in real property exchanges should ensure strict compliance with the identification and receipt requirements of Section 1031, including the 45-day identification period and the 180-day exchange period. Qualified intermediaries are commonly used to facilitate the exchange and avoid actual or constructive receipt of exchange proceeds. Taxpayers who exchanged personal property under the pre-TCJA rules should review their depreciation schedules and consider whether any remaining deferred gains from prior exchanges will be triggered upon disposition of the personal property.
