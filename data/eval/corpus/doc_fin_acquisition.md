# Accounting for Business Combinations with Goodwill under ASC 805

**Document ID:** doc_fin_acquisition.md
**Owner:** Corporate Accounting Department
**Last updated:** July 12, 2026

## Overview of Business Combination Accounting

ASC 805 (Business Combinations) establishes the principles for recognizing and measuring assets acquired, liabilities assumed, and any noncontrolling interest in a business combination. Under the acquisition method, the acquirer is required to identify the acquiree, determine the acquisition date, recognize and measure identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed at their fair values, and recognize goodwill or a gain from a bargain purchase.

## The Acquisition Method

When Meridian Analytics acquires another business, the acquisition method requires the purchase price to be allocated to the identifiable net assets acquired based on their fair values at the acquisition date. The purchase price includes the fair value of consideration transferred, which may include cash, equity interests, or contingent consideration. Acquisition-related costs such as legal fees, due diligence costs, and investment banking fees are expensed as incurred and are not included in the purchase price.

## Treatment of Excess Purchase Price over Fair Value

In a **business combination** where the **purchase price** exceeds the **fair value** of **identifiable net assets**, the residual amount is recorded as **goodwill**. For example, if an acquirer pays $10 million for a target whose identifiable net assets have a fair value of $8 million, the $2 million excess is recorded as goodwill. This treatment under ASC 805 reflects the principle that goodwill represents the future economic benefits arising from assets that are not individually identified and separately recognized, such as assembled workforce, customer relationships, and synergies expected from the combination.

## Measurement Period Adjustments

ASC 805 provides for a measurement period, not to exceed one year from the acquisition date, during which the acquirer may adjust the provisional amounts recognized for a business combination. These adjustments reflect additional information obtained about facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date. If the acquirer obtains information about the fair value of identifiable net assets during this period that changes the residual goodwill amount, retroactive adjustments are made. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that it may be impaired.

## Goodwill Impairment Testing

Under ASC 350 (Intangibles -- Goodwill and Other), goodwill impairment testing may be performed using either a qualitative assessment or a quantitative impairment test. The qualitative assessment evaluates whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount. If the qualitative assessment indicates that impairment is more likely than not, a quantitative test is performed comparing the reporting unit's fair value to its carrying amount, including goodwill. Any excess of carrying amount over fair value is recognized as an impairment loss, up to the amount of goodwill allocated to that reporting unit. Proper documentation of goodwill valuation and impairment testing is essential for compliance with US GAAP.
